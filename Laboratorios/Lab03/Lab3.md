Q1. Which are the significant frequencies for EMG acquisitions? Are they the same in all body areas such as facial
area? 
For EMG acquisitions, the relevant part of the signal is found between 20-500 Hz. However, they are not exactly the same in all body areas, since it can
vary depending on the size of the muscle and thickness of the tissue. In the facial area, for example, the muscles are smaller and the recordings of the 
signal can be affected by artifacts and eye movements, so the frequency distribution may be different.

Q2. Which kind of filter is essential when working with EMG signals? Why do we need to apply such a filter?
A band-pass filter, because the relevant signal has a range between 20-500 Hz. It is necessary to apply that kind of filter 
since it can remove low-frequencies caused by artifacts and the movement of electrodes and high frequencies that result from
electrical noise while preserving the important parts of the signal.

Q3. How does the amplitude differ in each muscular contraction? Is there a difference for body locations?
The amplitude tends to increase with the intensity of the muscular contraction; a weak contraction would produce a 
low-amplitude signal, and the signal would be close to the baseline during relaxation. This amplitude can change depending on the
body location, because different muscles have different characteristics and can produce stronger o weaker signals.
